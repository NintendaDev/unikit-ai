## Active Summary

<!-- unikit:active-summary:start -->
Goal: show a new player, within the first two minutes, that a locked door opens by a nearby switch.
Scope: the first room, one door, one switch and a hint arrow. Not covered: sound, later rooms, balance.
Constraints: one finger control; no text windows.
Decisions: DEC-1 — the arrow points at the switch, not at the door; DEC-2 — the switch glows until it is used; DEC-3 — one full variant only.
Risks: RISK-1 — the arrow is read as a quest marker and the room is ignored; RISK-2 — the glow gives the answer away.
Open questions: OQ-1 — how long a new player looks around before touching anything; OQ-2 — whether the arrow should fade after the first door; OQ-3 — a second, longer room is left for a later experiment.
Success signals: a new player finds the switch without help; the door opens within the first two minutes; nobody asks what to do.
Next step: build an experiment card from this research.

## Conclusions

DEC-1 — the arrow points at the switch, because the door is already in view and the switch is the unknown part.
DEC-2 — the switch glows until it is used, so a player who ignores the arrow still has a second cue.
DEC-3 — one full variant only; a shorter variant is dropped by the designer.

RISK-1 — the arrow is read as a quest marker and the room is ignored.
RISK-2 — the glow gives the answer away, and finding the switch stops being a discovery.

## Findings

The first room is small and closed. The player stands near the door and the switch is behind a low wall. Nothing in the room says that the switch matters. The measurement of the test was left open: no study of how many players are available has been made.
